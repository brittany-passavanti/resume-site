Section 06

When Users Push Back on AI

Resistance isn't a problem to solve — it's a human response to uncertainty. The job isn't to convince skeptics. It's to find the people who are already curious and teach them how to bring others along.

What Resistance Usually Is

  • Fear of being replaced or exposed as not tech-savvy
  • No clear answer to "what's in it for me personally"
  • A bad first experience that never got addressed
  • No internal champion making it feel safe to try

What It's Almost Never

  • A fundamental objection to AI itself
  • Something that needs more product features to fix
  • A CSM problem to solve alone
  • Permanent — resistance moves when the right person models the behavior
The Approach — Find the Champion, Teach Them to Lead
Every account has someone who's already curious. My job is to find that person early, invest in them specifically, and equip them to have the conversations I can't have at scale. I'm not the one who changes minds inside an organization — the right internal voice is. I just give them the language, the confidence, and the wins to point to.
  • Identify the champion in the first 30 days. It's rarely the admin. Look for the person who's already experimenting — they show up in the usage data before they raise their hand.
  • Give them a quick win they can share. One specific example of AI saving them real time. Something concrete enough that they can walk into a team meeting and say "look what I did." That story travels without me.
  • Teach them how to handle the skeptics — not by debating AI, but by modeling comfort with it. Skeptics don't move on argument. They move when someone they trust stops being afraid.
  • Normalize the learning curve out loud. The best thing a champion can say to a resister is "I didn't get it at first either." That permission matters more than any feature demo.
  • Track it in the health score. User maturity distribution — how many people are moving from passive to active — is the leading indicator that champion activation is working.
If Paul Asks How You Scale This Across a Large Book
"I can't be in every conversation — and I shouldn't be. My job is to build the conditions where adoption spreads internally. That means investing deeply in the right person early, giving them tools to lead, and using the health score to tell me which accounts need that intervention and which ones are already moving on their own."
Section 07

Scenario Bank

The Ghost Account (Gingr)

  • Client completed kickoff, then had zero logins in the first two weeks.
  • Caught it in usage data and reached out with a specific re-engagement offer instead of a generic check-in.
  • The real issue was that the owner had told staff about it, but never actually showed them how to use it.
  • One short session got them activated, and they renewed.
  • Takeaway: zero logins in week two is a fire alarm.

The 2-Person Island (Gingr)

  • Client had two to three power users, and the rest of the team was uninvolved.
  • Recognized that if either champion left, the account would be immediately at risk.
  • Built them a simple internal rollout guide and offered a group training they could host themselves.
  • Takeaway: enable the champions to bring their team along instead of staying in every conversation yourself.

The Unused Budget (Indeed)

  • Enterprise client bought a large block of sponsored job spend and had barely touched it months in.
  • Caught it in the utilization dashboard before they said anything.
  • The real issue was that the TA team had not been looped in after the contract was signed, so the budget existed but no one owned running it.
  • Worked with their internal champion to get the right people in the room, built a utilization plan, and got them active before renewal.
  • Takeaway: unutilized spend is a churn signal, so get ahead of it with a concrete plan, not a check-in.

The Pilot Rollout (Gingr Enterprise)

  • Five-hundred to one-thousand employee client rolling software across multiple locations.
  • Some rollouts were all at once, others were phased by pilot location.
  • Managed triage directly with product and engineering on their behalf around custom dashboards, integrations, and software issues.
  • Takeaway: enterprise adoption is not one event, it is a coordinated sequence, and someone has to own the thread.
